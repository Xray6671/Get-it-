01 — Do I Need This?

What Nevada requires

Count everyone on payroll, including part-time and seasonal workers.

11+

11 or more employees

You need a written workplace safety program that includes a training program. (Nevada Revised Statutes, NRS 618.383)

26+

26 or more employees

You also need a safety committee, with members, duties and a meeting schedule.

30

Workers exposed to heat

If most workers in a job are exposed to heat for more than 30 minutes in any hour, you also need a written heat hazard analysis, heat provisions in your safety program, a designated person and trained employees. (Nevada’s heat-illness regulation, R131-24)

Heat rules cover hot kitchens, laundries, warehouses and vehicles, not just outdoor work. Employees working indoors in air conditioning are not covered unless the air conditioning fails. Employers with 10 or fewer employees still must protect workers from heat.

04 — Crew Training

What your team learns

A 45-minute lesson included with the Heat Plan and Full Safety Program. Up to 25 people per session, with Spanish handouts and signed attendance records.

By the end, each employee can

  • Name the signs of heat exhaustion and heat stroke
  • Explain why confusion means calling 911
  • Find the water, shade and cooling on their site
  • Follow the break schedule at each heat level
  • Explain why new and returning workers start with shorter days
  • Name their designated person and give 911 the work address
  • Rest when they feel sick from heat, without discipline

Lesson steps

  • 0–5 min: Why it matters
  • 5–15 min: Signs of heat illness
  • 15–25 min: Your company’s plan: water, shade, breaks, designated person
  • 25–30 min: Shorter first days for new and returning workers
  • 30–40 min: Emergency practice: call, cool, meet the ambulance
  • 40–45 min: Questions, a quick check and sign-in

Designated persons get an extra 30–45 minutes on reading the heat index, the daily heat log, changing or stopping work, and emergency response.

05 — Questions

Safety & heat questions

Is a template from the internet enough?

A template is a starting point. Nevada expects your plan to match your actual workplace, including a written hazard analysis for your jobs. Our kit walks you through that, and our done-for-you service builds it from a walkthrough.

Do you work outside Las Vegas?

Yes. We serve employers across Nevada. Walkthroughs can be on site or by video call.

Can you train my crew in Spanish?

Our handouts come in English and Spanish, and training is given in a language each employee understands, as Nevada requires.

What about temporary workers from a staffing agency?

Nevada requires employers who use a temporary employment service to give those workers safety training before they start at each site. We include this in your program.

Is this legal advice?

No. This is workplace safety consulting and training. You remain responsible for carrying out your plans, and you may have your attorney review any plan.

Are you connected to Nevada OSHA?

No. Nevada Business Watch is a private company, not affiliated with Nevada OSHA or any government agency.
